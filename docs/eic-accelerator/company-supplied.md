## IP notes (to confirm with a Norwegian IP lawyer; this is not legal advice)

1. The operator's statement that the author owns the IP is recorded as stated, not as verified.
   Norway has specific rules for software and inventions made inside an employment relationship.
   Counsel should confirm who owns what, for each author and each period.
2. If the authors hold the IP personally, the company needs a written assignment, or an exclusive
   licence, before the full proposal. An applicant that does not control its IP fails the IP
   criterion.
3. A large part of ARIA's code was written in sessions with an AI assistant. Counsel should
   advise how authorship and ownership of AI assisted output are documented (human direction,
   review and selection are recorded in the repository history).
4. No patent has been filed. A software implemented invention is patentable in Europe only with a
   technical effect. The evidence bound verification architecture is a candidate to assess with a
   patent attorney. Do not disclose it publicly before that assessment (EIC proposals are
   confidential, public talks and repositories are not).
5. Protection that exists today: copyright in the software and trade secret in a private
   repository. The strategy section should say this plainly and add the assignment, the patent
   assessment and a dependency licence inventory (SBOM) as dated work.

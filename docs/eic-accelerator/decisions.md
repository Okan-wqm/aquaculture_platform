# Decisions the company takes before submitting (from the official documents)

Written 2026-10-26. References are to `official-requirements.md`.

## 1. There is no last day; the cost of a failed submission is high

The short proposal can be submitted at any time. The 3 November date is only the next monthly
batching (the following ones are 1 December 2026 and 5 January 2027). **Three unsuccessful
submissions close the Accelerator for the legal entity for the rest of Horizon Europe**, and the
short proposal counts (section 5 of the digest). A rushed submission that gets a NO GO spends one of
the three.

## 2. The TRL test, read honestly

The criterion is whether the innovation "has completed all aspects of TRL 5 (validation in a
relevant environment)". The independent TRL assessment in `trl-justification.md` reads the
maintenance pipeline as **TRL 4 as a whole**, with TRL 5 (lower end) only for the plan to pull
request segment, on one example (n = 1). The weakest link is the finding stage: 0 of 13 planted
real defects found. Two evaluators out of four giving NO GO is enough to reject.

What moves the reading to a clear TRL 5 (see `work-packages-24m.md` for the plan):

1. The change review path (D3) measured on the development set against a plain language model.
2. At least three clean measured cycles (gate G0) in observe mode, which needs the Board
   enrolment signature and the decision to switch observe mode on.
3. The measured numbers with n and confidence intervals, quoted with their limits.

## 3. Recommendation on timing

Submit when the TRL 5 evidence exists, not on 3 November. A realistic window is the December or
January batch, if the three items above are done. The cost of waiting is four weeks per batch;
the cost of an early NO GO is permanent. The company decides.

## 4. Funding type

| Option          | What the official text requires                                                                                                                                                         | Fit today                                           |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Grant only      | Evidence of sufficient financial means or access to resources to commercialise and scale; need for the grant; 30% of eligible costs from own resources; once only for the legal entity. | Needs proof of funds the company has not stated.    |
| Blended finance | The grant (70% of eligible costs) plus an equity investment of EUR 1 to 10 million, at most 25% of voting shares.                                                                       | Needs a decision on dilution and an investor story. |
| Investment only | TRL 9 style deployment and scale up.                                                                                                                                                    | Does not fit.                                       |

Norway is a Horizon Europe Associated Country. If its association agreement excludes the EIC
Fund investment component, Norwegian applicants can only apply for grant only (WP p.176). Ask the
national contact point (Innovation Norway) which applies.

## 5. Things only the company can do

1. Register on the Funding and Tenders Portal and obtain a validated PIC (needed before the form
   can be started).
2. Confirm the legal entity is a start-up or SME established in Norway or an EU country.
3. Settle IP ownership with a Norwegian IP lawyer (see `company-supplied.md`).
4. Decide the funding type and the amount (the lump sum is built from the cost estimate).
5. Supply the market, revenue, team, investor and financing facts. The business model is not scored
   at the short stage, but two to four deck slides should show business model, target market and
   growth forecast.
6. Record the video (up to three team members) and sign the AI use disclosure.
7. Prepare a live demonstration: at the full stage the technology expert checks claims and may
   see a prototype.
